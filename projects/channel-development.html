Channel Development

From a single sales route to several, including a B2B channel developed from scratch

A manufacturer selling through one route reaches exactly the buyers that route reaches. I opened and coordinated additional sales channels — marketplaces, the company’s own site and a business-to-business distributor route — and built the pricing scenario behind each one.

Driftwood Channel development and coordination 2020 – 2022 Project overview

Context

Every channel wants something different: a marketplace wants listings, images and a price that survives its commission; a distributor wants volume, lead times and a margin; the company’s own site wants the whole customer journey. Adding a channel is not a sales decision alone — it is a product-information, logistics and pricing problem first.

My contribution

  1. Developed and obtained a business-to-business distributor channel, the route that did not exist before and that sells in volume rather than by the unit.
  2. Coordinated channel requirements — product information, listings, renders and descriptions — so the same product could be presented correctly in more than one place.
  3. Built the pricing scenario behind each route, including the distributor scenario, inside the product cost models rather than as a separate exercise.
  4. Kept the routes consistent with each other, so one product did not contradict itself between two places a customer could find it.

A specific 2023 campaign aimed at construction companies was run by a colleague and the commercial department. What is mine is the channel development described above.

Result

Reach

Multiple routes to market operating in parallel.

Consumer marketplaces, the company’s own site and a distributor route ran alongside each other, each with its own price scenario.

New route

A business-to-business channel that did not exist before.

Developed and obtained, priced on volume rather than per unit, and modelled inside the same cost structure as every consumer route.
