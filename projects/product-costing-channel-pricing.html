Costing & Pricing

One manufacturing cost, resolved into five channel prices

I built the cost models behind a furniture range — down to board yield, edge banding and hardware — and the pricing structure that carried a single manufacturing cost into every sales channel it was sold through.

Driftwood Product costing and channel pricing 2021 – 2022 Project overview

Context

The same product was sold through several marketplaces, a distributor route and the company’s own site. Each one takes a different commission, carries a different logistics cost and is treated differently for tax — so one manufacturing cost has to become several prices, and the cost itself depends on board yield, edge banding, hardware and packaging.

My contribution

  1. Built the itemised cost model per product code — materials, cutting list, board yield, waste, edge banding, hardware, packaging and consumables — rather than costing at the level of a finished product.
  2. Built the per-channel price scenarios inside those same files, so commission, transport and tax treatment were applied to one shared cost base instead of to five separate guesses.
  3. Maintained the models across the range and across two years, product code by product code, as materials and channels changed.
  4. Prepared the itemised budgets that the range’s pricing conversations were held against.

The workbook template itself was not mine — it was created inside the company before this work — and pricing decisions were taken by the business. What is mine is the cost model built inside it and the channel structure applied to it.
